- **Connect drawer: own the drawer so its gestures and the content's stay apart** ([`d4e8e2064`](https://github.com/urnetwork/android/commit/d4e8e20642c3748b87e250e909bc755878bea584))
  The Material sheet could not keep the drawer's open/close gesture and the content's scroll separate: while its settle animation runs it consumes any touch's down for its own drag, so a scroll that started right after an opening flick went to a sheet already at the top and died there, and a pull through the content fought the animation frame by frame so only a fast flick closed the drawer. Nothing in the sheet state can move, snap or interrupt it from outside.
- **Connect drawer: opening, closing and scrolling the content are separate gestures** ([`67f76b828`](https://github.com/urnetwork/android/commit/67f76b828490925ee944f7e3126f20ff96963ab9))
  The Material sheet hands a gesture across the boundary in both directions: a drag that opened the drawer ran on into scrolling its content, and a content scroll that reached the top ran on into closing the drawer. Each touch is now one gesture of one kind. A touch that lands while the drawer is anywhere but settled open is a drawer gesture: once the drawer reaches the top the rest of the movement and the release fling go nowhere. A touch on the settled-open drawer is a content gesture ...
- **Connect drawer: the content is back at the top whenever the drawer closes** ([`6cab035a0`](https://github.com/urnetwork/android/commit/6cab035a0907a0c6badab1abdd8a7bca7cc7f543))
  The reset watched the sheet's current value and fired one animateScrollTo when it flipped to the peek. On the device it never took: reopening the drawer after closing it by the handle, by the Connect tab or by a fling showed the content where it had been left, so the collapsed peek showed the wrong rows.
- **Connect drawer: the expanded drawer stops two thirds of the way up** ([`d036368d7`](https://github.com/urnetwork/android/commit/d036368d74d2d87b16da806727923d252b2b1d1f))
  The Material sheet expanded to the whole screen once the drawer's content outgrew it, so the drag handle ended up under the status bar and the connect graphic disappeared behind the sheet. Cap the scrolling content at two thirds of the screen less the drag handle: the drawer now opens to that height, leaving the top of the connect graphic exposed above it (as the iOS sheet's fixed expanded height does), and the content scrolls to its end within it.
- **Connect drawer: keep the sheet's own open behaviour, no fling carry-over** ([`b1f79c7e9`](https://github.com/urnetwork/android/commit/b1f79c7e97a686a6cce29961f580ff9eafc7be88))
  Reverts the two carry-over changes. A flick that opens the drawer leaves the content at its top again, as the Material sheet does: the report was about the scroll inside the open drawer, not about the opening flick, and the carry-over read as an extra scroll after every fast release.
- **Connect drawer: the content continues the fling on its own clock, not after the sheet stops** ([`0626c042e`](https://github.com/urnetwork/android/commit/0626c042ed2365c4be0724b25ba684f11ae8b1bb))
  The carry-over waited for the sheet's expand animation to finish and only then flung the content with the leftover velocity. The sheet rises on a fixed 300 ms tween that eases to a full stop, so the content setting off afterwards read as a second, phantom flick: "the drawer scrolls after a drag stops as if a flick happened" (S21 recording and touch trace).
- **Connect drawer: a flick that opens the drawer carries on into its content** ([`e5854f33d`](https://github.com/urnetwork/android/commit/e5854f33de3fdd8759e766f3396e95833d88dde1))
  A flick from the collapsed drawer expanded the sheet and stopped dead with the content at its top, cut just below the transport bar, and a second flick was needed to read on. The Material sheet's nested-scroll connection consumes the whole fling to settle at the expanded anchor, so the content never saw any of it. Reproduced on the S21 from a screen recording and the touch trace: every pull in the report was a ~100 ms flick, not a drag.
